# The generic "made by AI" look

What makes a page look as if a model or a template designed it, why that matters, and how to replace defaults with decisions without losing what works. The look comes from defaults: the most common font, color, layout, component kit, copy and motion, chosen because nothing more specific was decided. Sameness is not a usability failure by itself, but it costs distinctiveness and trust, and it often travels with real defects: weak contrast in dark themes, invented content and deceptive patterns.

**Load when:** the user asks whether a page looks AI-made, generic, templated or "vibe-coded"; the project was generated with an AI builder or coding agent; or a build or redesign is planned with an AI tool.
**Skip when:** the brand is clearly established and nobody asked about distinctiveness.
**Pair with (when present):** `core/brand-preservation`, `core/content-and-trust`, `patterns/visual-scale`, `patterns/sales-journey`, `patterns/component-libraries`, `patterns/visual-styles`, `patterns/motion`.

## Objectives

The page looks like it belongs to this business and could not be mistaken for any other: its type, color, imagery, layout and words come from the subject, the audience and the brand. Every number, logo, review and claim is real. Conventions that help people (where navigation sits, what a button looks like) are kept; only the arbitrary defaults are replaced.

## Priority principles

1. **Defects first, sameness second.** Contrast failures, invented content and deceptive patterns are findings at their normal priority. Looking generic on its own is P3: a distinctiveness opportunity, not a defect.
2. **Never fabricate to look less generic.** Replace placeholder logos, statistics, testimonials and photos with real ones or remove them; never invent new ones.
3. **Keep conventions, replace defaults.** Familiar navigation, form and button patterns help people; a default font, palette or card grid chosen for no reason does not.
4. **Specific beats novel.** The fix is a decision grounded in the subject (its materials, place, people, words), not a random unusual effect.
5. **Respect a chosen look.** If the owner chose Inter and indigo deliberately, that is the brand; report the observation once and move on.

## Checks

### Defects that often come with the generic look (normal priority)
- **Invented content:** testimonials from people who do not exist, stock or AI faces presented as customers, logo walls of companies that are not clients, round-number user counts ("10,000+ teams"), star ratings with no source, "as seen in" with no coverage. These are deceptive and, in many places, unlawful (`core/content-and-trust`).
- **Deceptive patterns:** a 2025 study of web components generated by language models found at least one deceptive pattern in more than half of them, most often visual interference: color steering toward one choice, hidden information or invented urgency. Check every generated pricing, cookie, checkout and cancellation component (`patterns/pricing-and-persuasion`). A typical generated case: one plan with a glow and a gradient button, the others with faint gray "Choose" links.
- **Dark-theme contrast:** permanent dark themes with medium-gray body text and thin weights frequently fail 4.5:1 (`patterns/dark-mode-and-themes`).
- **Gradient text and text over gradients** failing contrast at their weakest point.
- **Placeholder leftovers:** dummy Latin filler text, "Acme", example.com links, "Your Company", default favicons, buttons and forms that do nothing.
- **Emoji as the only label** for navigation or features, which screen readers announce by their emoji names.

### Signals of default-driven design (P3 unless they cause a defect above)

| Area | Common default | What a decision looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Type** | Inter or a system sans for everything; one headline word in serif italic; tracked all-caps eyebrow labels above every heading; monospace for small labels | A typeface chosen for the subject and audience; one or two families with a clear role each; emphasis used once where it matters |
| **Color** | Indigo or violet primary; purple-to-blue gradients; near-black background with one neon accent; colored glows and shadows | A palette from the brand, the product or its setting; gradients only where they mean something |
| **Hero** | Centered headline with a small pill badge above it, a gradient word, two buttons, and an abstract blob or dashboard screenshot | A first screen that shows the real product, place, people or result, with the one primary action (`patterns/sales-journey`) |
| **Sections** | Three or four identical icon cards; "1, 2, 3" steps; a stat banner; a logo strip; a testimonial carousel; an FAQ; a gradient call-to-action band, in that order | Sections chosen for what this audience needs to decide, in the order they need it; numbered steps only for a real sequence |
| **Components** | One radius and one soft shadow on everything; thin gray borders; colored top or left borders on cards; frosted-glass cards; component-kit defaults untouched | Tokens set from the brand (`patterns/component-libraries`); depth and shape used to show hierarchy |
| **Motion** | Every section fades and slides up on scroll; every card lifts on hover | One purposeful motion moment; motion that responds to what the user does (`patterns/motion`) |
| **Imagery** | Abstract 3D shapes, gradient blobs, generic stock, AI illustrations with telltale artifacts | Real photos of the product, place, team and customers; illustration in one consistent hand |
| **Icons** | One outline icon set at one size on every card | Icons only where they help recognition, consistent with the brand |
| **Copy** | "Unlock", "elevate", "seamless", "supercharge", "all-in-one platform", "built for the modern…", three-word taglines with periods; claims any competitor could make | The business's own nouns, numbers, places and promises; specific outcomes; plain words (`core/content-and-trust`) |

- Several signals together, especially in the hero and copy, are what make a page read as generated; one alone is usually just a common choice.
- Check the page against a competitor or two: if the hero could swap logos with them, report it.

### When the skill builds or redesigns
- Decide type, color, imagery and the one bold element before writing code, from the subject and the brand inventory.
- Replace component-kit theme tokens instead of adding on top of them.
- Use the real content the owner provides; where content is missing, leave a clearly marked gap for the owner, not invented text.

## Anti-patterns

- Replacing the generic look with another trend (a random brutalist or neon treatment) that has nothing to do with the business.
- Swapping invented testimonials for different invented testimonials.
- Calling a page "AI-generated" as an accusation; report the specific signals and their cost.
- Removing familiar conventions (top navigation, standard buttons) to look different.
- Rating sameness as P1 or P2 when nothing is broken.

## Exceptions and context

- **Internal tools and dashboards** benefit from conventional, quiet design; sameness there is mostly fine.
- **Early prototypes and tests** may use defaults on purpose; say what to decide before launch.
- **Brands built on a minimal sans-serif look** are not generic because they are simple; check for decisions in imagery, copy and detail instead.

## Implementation cautions

- Safe UI work: removing placeholder leftovers, fixing contrast, removing emoji-only labels, removing invented logos, statistics and testimonials (with the owner told what was removed and why), reducing repeated entrance animations.
- Functional work, recommend only: a new typeface, palette or illustration style, new photography, rewriting positioning copy, restructuring page sections. These are brand decisions for the owner.
- Never generate fake people, quotes, logos or numbers, even as placeholders that could ship.
